MODEL PROPOSAL — qualitative interpretation, not an established result
hypothesis: Short-horizon close momentum expressed as the trailing close-to-close return with the baseline lookback and direction retained, now divided by the trailing daily-return standard deviation over the smallest permitted volatility window. The motivation is the documented cross-symbol dispersion in one-observation return scale and in the unnormalized score scale, which otherwise lets high-dispersion symbols dominate any cross-sectional reading. Scaling by a strictly positive trailing volatility is hypothesized, but not proven, to make scores more comparable across symbols. The volatility-scaled score is not an annual Sharpe ratio and does not remove systematic risk. This is an exploratory development hypothesis on a small convenience sample: usefulness, direction and any benefit remain unproven, and these observations can never serve as a holdout.
Fact references: spec, units, 300750.XSHE.sample_sd_score, 600036.XSHG.sample_sd_score, 601318.XSHG.sample_sd_score
rationale: The revision changes only the normalization: lookback and direction stay as assigned, the return-magnitude exclusion stays unset because close-only jumps cannot identify corporate actions, and no applicability exclusion beyond the cited findings is added. Signals still use only closes observed at or before the signal close, absent dates stay unfilled, and execution is no earlier than the next observed close; counts of signals with a next observed close do not establish tradability. The chosen volatility window adds no trailing-observation requirement beyond the return lookback, so warm-up and eligible-signal counts match the baseline in the receipts, and no window was excluded for non-positive volatility. A signal additionally requires a strictly positive trailing volatility, so an all-zero return window would be ineligible rather than divided by a non-positive denominator. Costs and uncertainty: one added parameter that must be justified from cited evidence, a changed score scale whose cross-symbol comparability benefit is hypothesized but not demonstrated, and rendered comparisons that are descriptive only and establish no improvement. No alpha, superiority or removal of systematic risk is claimed. A future independent evaluation would need forward labels on genuinely held-out data, a point-in-time universe, corporate-action handling, transaction costs and bid/ask information before any benefit could be claimed.
